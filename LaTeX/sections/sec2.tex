\section{Action, geometry, and fixed data}
\label{sec:revised2}

We use an orthonormal Euclidean metric $\delta_{ab}$ and orientation $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$. The constants $\kappa,L$ are positive; $\alpha,\theta$ are real. No cosmological term, matter source, or boundary is added. The main action contains the squared norm $I_{\mathrm{W}}$ of the irreducible EC-Weyl tensor $C_{\mathrm{EC}}$, whose projection is defined in Section~\ref{sec:ecweyl}. We call this the irreducible EC-Weyl action. The LC-Weyl action replaces only this quadratic term by the squared norm $I_{\mathrm{LC}}=(C_{\mathrm{LC}})_{abcd}(C_{\mathrm{LC}})^{abcd}$; the EC Ricci scalar and complete NY form remain common. Section~\ref{sec:ricci-tracefree} compares the Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action. These names specify the quadratic term, not a change of the entire connection.

\begin{equation}
S=\int_M\left[\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{EC}}}{2\kappa^2}
+\alpha I_{\mathrm{W}}\right)\mathrm{vol}_4+\theta N\right],
\qquad U=-S\big|_{\mathrm{ansatz}}.
\tag{A}\label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
$I_{\mathrm{W}}=(C_{\mathrm{EC}})_{abcd}(C_{\mathrm{EC}})^{abcd}$. Selecting this curvature sector is a model choice, not a claim that other sectors are physically forbidden. The sign convention for $U$ is explicit: it is minus the restricted action, not a Lorentzian Hamiltonian. Our local conventions are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
de^a&=\frac12 C^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,
&\omega^a{}_b&=\Gamma^a{}_{bc}e^c,\\
T^a&=de^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b,
&R^a{}_b&=d\omega^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b,\\
T^a&=\frac12 T^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,
&R_{ab}&=\frac12 R_{abcd}e^c\wedge e^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C}\label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
The curvature--coframe term contains one two-form and two one-forms, giving coefficient $1/2$; the product of two torsion two-forms gives $1/4$.\cite{NiehYan1982,Chandia1997}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N&=d(e^a\wedge T_a)=T^a\wedge T_a-R_{ab}\wedge e^a\wedge e^b,\\
\frac{N}{\mathrm{vol}_4}
&=\frac14\epsilon^{abcd}T^e{}_{ab}T_{ecd}
-\frac12\epsilon^{abcd}R_{abcd}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NY}\label{eq:NY}
\end{equation}
Define the scalar coefficients of the two four-forms by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T\,\mathrm{vol}_4&=T^a\wedge T_a,\\
\mathcal R\,\mathrm{vol}_4&=R_{ab}\wedge e^a\wedge e^b,\\
N&=(\mathcal T-\mathcal R)\mathrm{vol}_4.
\end{aligned}\tag{NYD}\label{eq:NYD}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal R$ is the curvature--coframe contraction, not the Ricci scalar. The code selectors are related to these coefficients in Appendix B. Unless a comparison is stated explicitly, $U$ denotes the reduced potential of the irreducible EC-Weyl action.

Introduce the volume-preserving coframe below. We use $r$ for the scale of all three manifolds; it is the parameter called $R$ by the Nil engine. The round sphere has $x=1$.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e^0&=r s^{1/3}\sigma^0,&e^1&=r s^{1/3}\sigma^1,\\
e^2&=r s^{-2/3}\sigma^2,&e^3&=L\,du,\\
s&=1+\varepsilon>0,&x&=s^{-2/3}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{F}\label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
de^0&=n_0 e^1\wedge e^2,&de^1&=n_1 e^2\wedge e^0,\\
de^2&=n_2 e^0\wedge e^1,&de^3&=0,\\
(n_0,n_1,n_2)_{S^3}&=\frac1r(4/x,4/x,4x^2),\\
(n_0,n_1,n_2)_{T^3}&=(0,0,0),
\qquad (n_0,n_1,n_2)_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}=\frac1r(0,0,-x^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{G}\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
For $S^3$ we use SU(2), not a further quotient; its round physical radius is $r/2$. The spatial angles of $T^3$ have period $2\pi$. The circle coordinate $u$ has period one, so $L$ is its circumference. For Nil use $\sigma^0=dy^0$, $\sigma^1=dy^1$, $\sigma^2=dz-y^0dy^1$, with periods $(2\pi,2\pi,4\pi^2)$ and the $y^0$-face gluing $z\mapsto z+2\pi y^1$. Hold this quotient fixed when varying the scale and shape.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Vol}_4=c_M Lr^3,
\qquad c_{S^3}=\frac{\pi^2}{4},\quad
c_{T^3}=(2\pi)^3,\quad c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}=(2\pi)^4.
\tag{VOL}\label{eq:VOL}
\end{equation}
The geometric torsion is prescribed by
\begin{equation}
T_{abc}=\frac{2\eta}{r}\epsilon_{abc}\big|_{a,b,c<3}
+\frac13(\delta_{ac}V_b-\delta_{ab}V_c),
\qquad V_b=V\delta_{b3}.
\tag{T}\label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
The spatial epsilon term is present only when all its indices are below three. We fix signed $\eta,V$ and vary $r$ and shape: the axial frame coefficient $2\eta/r$ is not held fixed as $r$ varies. It is the same in all three spatial frame directions and does not depend on shape; this is part of the definition of the family. Referring it instead to one shape-dependent length, as the preceding study does on its anisotropic torus paths,\cite{paper1} defines a different family that agrees with (T) only at $x=1$. The public engine retains its signed input array $\tau$; the dictionary is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{geom}}&=-\tau_{\mathrm{code}},
& (\eta,V)&=(-\eta_{\tau},-V_{\tau}),\\
K_{abc}[T]&=-\frac12(T_{abc}+T_{bca}-T_{cab}),
&K_{abc}[\tau]&=+\frac12(\tau_{abc}+\tau_{bca}-\tau_{cab}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{D}\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}

\section{Comparison with the torus and Nil families}
\label{sec:revised8}

For $T^3$ both LC-Weyl and complete NY vanish. $U$ is independent of $\alpha,\theta$, and shape. The shape independence relies on the shape-independent axial coefficient in (T), not on flatness alone. If either torsion amplitude is nonzero, it increases strictly with radius and has unattained infimum zero. If both vanish it is zero throughout, selecting neither a radius nor a shape.

For Nil and $\alpha\le0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}}{\partial x}
=\frac{c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}L}{3\kappa^2}
\left(3x^3r+\frac{32\kappa^2\beta x^7}{r}\right)>0
\qquad (\alpha=-\beta\le0).
\tag{NIL}\label{eq:NIL}
\end{equation}
There is no minimum at finite shape in the two-variable problem. The positive potential approaches infimum zero along $r=\ell t,x=t$, $t\to0^+$, with fixed positive length $\ell$. A separate radial minimum exists for negative $\alpha$ when shape is fixed:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A&=V^2,\quad B=9\eta^2+\frac34x^4,\quad D=4\kappa^2\beta x^8,\\
r_{\mathrm{slice}}^2
&=\frac{2D}{B+\sqrt{B^2+12AD}}
\qquad (\beta>0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{SLICE}\label{eq:SLICE}
\end{equation}
This expression includes $A=0$ but requires $\beta>0$. Releasing shape always lowers the potential in the direction specified by (NIL), so this radial minimum is not a two-variable minimum. Positive $\alpha$ is covered by Theorem 2.

At the common API amplitudes $V_\tau=4,\eta_\tau=-2$ and nonpositive $\alpha$, all three infima are zero and none is attained at an interior point. These inputs illustrate comparison among three unattained infima. For $V\ne0,\eta^2<4$, the negative attained spherical minimum can be compared with the two zero infima, but only for these specified quotients and fixed-amplitude paths, not as a universal topology-selection law. Vanishing local Nil curvature in a shape limit does not turn the fixed compact quotient into a torus.

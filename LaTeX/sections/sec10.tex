\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:revised10}

We have analysed static homogeneous reductions of the EC plus complete NY plus irreducible EC-Weyl-squared action in radius and volume-preserving shape. The complete NY density and form-index Pontryagin density vanish for the prescribed connection, and common conventions give explicit curvature invariants and potentials for the sphere, torus, and Nil families.

The tensor identity $C_{\mathrm{EC}}=C_{\mathrm{LC}}$ holds for every positive shape
in the prescribed torsion family. The two Weyl actions share the same reduced potential and
classification. The Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action includes two additional
curvature sectors and has the distinct potential shift (SHIFT); its effects
are not attributed to the irreducible Weyl sector.

The central spherical shape factorization reduces global minimization at nonpositive Weyl coupling to the round slice. For $V\ne0$ and $\eta^2<4$ there is a unique negative finite-radius minimum. Its radius and value are independent of the Weyl coupling, while the shape entry of the Hessian increases with the coupling's absolute value. The remaining parameter regions, including degenerate cases, separate unattained infima, a minimum set on the whole round ray, and a large-radius runaway.

Positive Weyl coupling gives small-radius unboundedness for nonround spheres and Nil. The torus potential is independent of shape and Weyl coupling, while monotonicity in Nil shape excludes a finite-shape two-variable minimum on the nonpositive side. Distinguishing fixed-shape radial minima from full shape minimization enables an analytic comparison of the extrema across the geometries.

These results identify how the curvature-squared term acts on radius and shape at fixed torsion parameters and circle circumference. Extensions to full-field vacua, time-dependent stability, and quantum transitions require the additional analysis of fields, constraints, and observables described in Section 9.

\section{Spherical shape bound and global minima}
\label{sec:revised5}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (shape bound for nonpositive coupling).} For $\alpha=-\beta\le0$ and fixed $L,\kappa,\eta,V$, the following identities hold for every $r,x>0$, including $\beta=0$.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
3-f(x)&=(x-1)^2(x^2+2x+3)\ge0,\\
U_{S^3}(r,x;-\beta)-U_{S^3}(r,1;-\beta)
&=\frac{c_{S^3}L}{3\kappa^2}(x-1)^2
\left[12r(x^2+2x+3)
+\frac{1024\kappa^2\beta x^2(x^2+x+1)^2}{r}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{B}\label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
The bracket is strictly positive, so equality occurs only at $x=1$. The EC term and the nonnegative Weyl term both support the round minimum at each fixed radius. At $\alpha=0$ the Weyl penalty vanishes at every shape, but the EC term still gives the strict shape difference above.

Global minimization over $r,x$ therefore reduces to
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
b&=9\eta^2-36,\qquad
U_{S^3}(r,1)=\frac{c_{S^3}L}{3\kappa^2}(V^2r^3+br),\\
r_*&=\frac{\sqrt{12-3\eta^2}}{\lvert V\rvert},
\qquad
U_*=-\frac{2c_{S^3}L V^2r_*^3}{3\kappa^2}
\quad(V\ne0,\ \eta^2<4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{MIN}\label{eq:MIN}
\end{equation}
When $V\ne0$ and $\eta^2<4$, the radial derivative crosses zero once from negative to positive. This is the unique global radial minimum, and (B) makes the shape unique as well. The restricted Hessian in $(r,\varepsilon)$ is positive definite:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_{rr}&=\frac{2c_{S^3}L V^2r_*}{\kappa^2}>0,
\qquad H_{r\varepsilon}=0,\\
H_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}
&=\frac{c_{S^3}L}{3\kappa^2}
\left(64r_*+\frac{8192\kappa^2\lvert\alpha\rvert}{r_*}\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{H}\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
The remaining degeneracies, unattained infima, and runaway cases are classified in Section 7. Here ``global'' always refers to the stated fixed data and the domain $r>0,x>0$.

Where the minimum exists, vanishing of the Weyl term on the round slice makes its radius and value independent of nonpositive $\alpha$. In contrast, the shape entry of (H) increases with $\lvert\alpha\rvert$. The selection of radius and the restoring curvature in the shape direction can therefore be read separately.

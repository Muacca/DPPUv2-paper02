\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:revised1}

\subsection{Problem and motivation}
Understanding a curvature-squared contribution to a homogeneous reduced
potential requires separating changes of overall radius from
volume-preserving changes of shape. Vanishing of the Weyl term on a round
slice and minimization with respect to deformations away from that slice
are distinct questions. We study their relation for the Einstein--Cartan (EC) action with the complete
Nieh--Yan (NY) term and an irreducible EC-Weyl-squared contribution on closed product manifolds.

A preceding study classifies radius potentials for EC plus complete NY at fixed
torsion parameters and circle circumference, distinguishing geometries and
variation paths.\cite{paper1} Here we add a volume-preserving shape variable
and compare Levi-Civita (LC) and irreducible EC-Weyl corrections to that classification. The spatial
factors are $S^3$, $T^3$, and a fixed compact Heisenberg quotient;
local connections and global integration domains are specified separately.

\subsection{Main results}
The curvature of the prescribed EC connection consists of spatial
two-forms, giving a vanishing form-index Pontryagin density. The complete
NY density also vanishes. Thus the reduced potential is determined by
the EC curvature and torsion terms together with the Weyl term.
We prove a tensor identity equating irreducible EC-Weyl and LC-Weyl
curvature on the entire prescribed family. The actions built from their squared norms therefore yield the same reduced
potential and classification of minima on this family, without identifying the
unrestricted theories. We also compare an action built from the squared norm
of Ricci-tracefree EC curvature, isolating its additional curvature contributions.

The central result is an exact decomposition of the spherical potential
into its round value at the same radius and a nonnegative shape difference.
For nonpositive Weyl coupling, equality in the shape bound holds only
on the round slice. Global minimization in radius and shape reduces to
a cubic polynomial in one variable. This gives the existence conditions,
location, value, and restricted Hessian of the minimum analytically.
The Weyl term preserves its round radius and value while increasing
the curvature of the potential in the shape direction.

For positive coupling the inverse-radius term gives small-radius
unboundedness on nonround spheres and Nil. In contrast, the Weyl term
vanishes on the torus, while monotonicity in shape excludes a finite-shape
minimum for Nil at nonpositive coupling. We compare these results under
explicit local and global conventions, distinguishing attained minima
from infima approached in degenerate limits.

\subsection{Organization and scope}
Section 2 defines the action, geometry, and fixed data. Sections 3--4
derive the curvature identities and reduced potentials. Sections 5--7
establish the spherical shape bound, positive-coupling asymptotics, and
parameter classification; Section 8 compares the torus and Nil families.
Section 9 relates the variational scope to physical questions, and
Section 10 gives the conclusions. The appendices provide connection
details, reproduction procedures, proof details, and the relation to
the preceding version.

The minimization is restricted to static homogeneous families with
positive radius and shape. The fixed quantities include signed torsion
parameters and circle circumference. Full-field vacua and dynamical
stability are distinct questions, discussed in Section 9.

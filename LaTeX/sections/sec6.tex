\section{Positive coupling and unboundedness}
\label{sec:revised6}

\textbf{Theorem 2 (small-radius unboundedness).} For $\alpha>0$, $U$ diverges to minus infinity as $r\to0^+$ at any fixed $x\ne1$ for $S^3$, and any fixed $x>0$ for Nil. This holds for every fixed finite pair $\eta,V$.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{r\to0^+}rU_{S^3}(r,x)
&=-\frac{1024c_{S^3}L\alpha}{3}x^2(1-x^3)^2<0
\quad(x\ne1),\\
\lim_{r\to0^+}rU_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}(r,x)
&=-\frac{4c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}L\alpha}{3}x^8<0
\quad(x>0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{ASY}\label{eq:ASY}
\end{equation}
In (S3),(TN), the terms proportional to $r$ and $r^3$ vanish as the radius decreases, while the inverse-radius coefficient is negative under the stated conditions. A fixed nonround shape thus suffices to establish unboundedness. On the round spherical slice the Weyl term vanishes, so its radial behaviour is distinguished from the nonround asymptotics.

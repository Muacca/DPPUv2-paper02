\section{Weyl projections, curvature identities, and reduced potentials}
\label{sec:revised4}

\subsection{LC curvature as a reference}
Solving the torsion-free Cartan equations with metric compatibility yields the LC connection and the following scalar for arbitrary $n_i$ in (G). Changing from input-array signs to geometric torsion signs does not change these quadratic scalar terms.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{LC}}&=n_0n_1+n_1n_2+n_2n_0
-\frac12(n_0^2+n_1^2+n_2^2),\\
R_{\mathrm{EC}}&=R_{\mathrm{LC}}-\frac{6\eta^2}{r^2}-\frac23V^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{R}\label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
Using the four-dimensional LC identity $C^2=R_{abcd}R^{abcd}-2R_{ab}R^{ab}+R^2/3$ gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f(x)&=4x-x^4,\\
R_{\mathrm{LC},S^3}&=\frac{8f(x)}{r^2},
&C_{\mathrm{LC},S^3}^2&=\frac{1024x^2(1-x^3)^2}{3r^4},\\
R_{\mathrm{LC},T^3}&=0,&C_{\mathrm{LC},T^3}^2&=0,\\
R_{\mathrm{LC},\mathrm{Nil}^3}&=-\frac{x^4}{2r^2},
&C_{\mathrm{LC},\mathrm{Nil}^3}^2&=\frac{4x^8}{3r^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{W}\label{eq:W}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Irreducible EC-Weyl curvature and the LC identity}
\label{sec:ecweyl}
For a metric-compatible connection, curvature is antisymmetric in each
index pair, but pair-exchange symmetry and the torsion-free algebraic
Bianchi identity need not hold. We select the Weyl part of the standard
Riemann--Cartan decomposition.\cite{McNutt2024} In four Euclidean dimensions
this part has ten components, splitting into two five-dimensional Hodge
sectors. Define the following linear operations on the full EC curvature:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_{abcd}&=\tfrac12(R^{\mathrm{EC}}_{abcd}+R^{\mathrm{EC}}_{cdab}),\\
F_{abcd}&=\tfrac13(S_{abcd}+S_{acdb}+S_{adbc}),\qquad Q=S-F,\\
q_{bd}&=\delta^{ac}Q_{abcd},\qquad s=\delta^{bd}q_{bd},\\
(C_{\mathrm{EC}})_{abcd}&=Q_{abcd}-\tfrac12(\delta_{ac}q_{bd}-\delta_{ad}q_{bc}
-\delta_{bc}q_{ad}+\delta_{bd}q_{ac})
+\tfrac{s}{6}(\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}).
\end{aligned}\tag{WP}\label{eq:WP}
\end{equation}
Here $F$ is a four-form, and $Q$ has algebraic Riemann symmetries. The last
line is the unique orthogonal removal of the part reconstructed from its
Ricci tensor using the metric: contraction gives
$q_{bd}-(2q_{bd}+s\delta_{bd})/2+s\delta_{bd}/2=0$.
This defines a curvature sector without imposing torsion-free field equations.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (identity on the prescribed family).}
For all $r,x>0$ and all signed $\eta,V$ in (T),
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{EC}}=C_{\mathrm{LC}},\qquad I_{\mathrm{W}}=I_{\mathrm{LC}}.
\tag{ID}\label{eq:ID}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} Set $h=\eta/r$ and $q_0=h^2+V^2/9$.
Using the Cartan connection and contortion in Appendix A, for arbitrary
$n_0,n_1,n_2$ in (G) the independent components of
$\Delta R=R_{\mathrm{EC}}-R_{\mathrm{LC}}$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R_{ijkl}&=-q_0(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}),\\
\Delta R_{i3jk}&=-\tfrac{2hV}{3}\epsilon_{ijk},\\
\Delta R_{ijk3}&=0,\qquad\Delta R_{i3j3}=0,
\qquad i,j,k,l\in\{0,1,2\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{CD}\label{eq:CD}
\end{equation}
Other components follow from antisymmetry within each pair. The first line
is entirely Ricci/scalar curvature: its spatial Ricci tensor is
$-2q_0\delta_{ij}$, its $33$ component is zero, and its scalar is $-6q_0$;
substitution in the last line of (WP) gives zero. The pair-symmetric part
of the second line is the four-form $-hV\epsilon_{abcd}/3$, removed in $F$.
Its pair-antisymmetric part is removed in $S$. Thus the Weyl projection of
$\Delta R$ vanishes. The LC curvature is unchanged by the first two
operations in (WP), proving the tensor identity. No division by $h,V$ or
$x-1$ is used. Since (CD) is independent of the structure constants, the
proof covers every positive shape in all three geometries.\hfill$\square$

The identity concerns this static homogeneous family. It does not assert
equality of the LC-Weyl and irreducible EC-Weyl actions for general fields, of their unrestricted
variations, or of their time-dependent perturbation operators.

\subsection{Common reduced potentials for the two Weyl actions}

Substituting the volume and the vanishing complete NY density gives
\begin{equation}
U_{S^3}=\frac{c_{S^3}L}{3\kappa^2}
\left[V^2r^3+(9\eta^2-12f(x))r
-\frac{1024\kappa^2\alpha x^2(1-x^3)^2}{r}\right].
\tag{S3}\label{eq:S3}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U_{T^3}&=\frac{c_{T^3}L}{3\kappa^2}(V^2r^3+9\eta^2r),\\
U_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}&=\frac{c_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}L}{3\kappa^2}
\left[V^2r^3+\left(9\eta^2+\frac34x^4\right)r
-\frac{4\kappa^2\alpha x^8}{r}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{TN}\label{eq:TN}
\end{equation}
Near the round slice, the Weyl term starts at quadratic order in the shape deformation. Distinguishing the quadratic coefficient from the second derivative gives the following local expansion.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{LC},S^3}^2
&=\frac{1024\varepsilon^2(\varepsilon+2)^2}{3r^4(1+\varepsilon)^{16/3}}
=\frac{4096}{3r^4}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3),\\
\left.\frac{\partial^2 C^2}{\partial\varepsilon^2}\right|_0
&=\frac{8192}{3r^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{DERIV}\label{eq:DERIV}
\end{equation}


Equation (W) shows that the Weyl term vanishes on the round spherical slice and throughout the torus family, whereas Nil has positive Weyl curvature squared at every finite positive shape. This distinction governs the shape minimization and small-radius behaviour developed below.

\subsection{Comparison with the Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action}
\label{sec:ricci-tracefree}
Define $\widehat R_{\mathrm{EC}}$ by applying only the last, Ricci-removal operation of (WP)
directly to $R_{\mathrm{EC}}$, with its own Ricci tensor and scalar.
This is a valid projection onto the twenty-dimensional Ricci-tracefree
subspace, whereas $C_{\mathrm{EC}}$ has ten components. The hat denotes Ricci-trace removal, not the Weyl projection. Define its squared norm as $I_{\mathrm{tf}}=(\widehat R_{\mathrm{EC}})_{abcd}(\widehat R_{\mathrm{EC}})^{abcd}$, where tf denotes zero Ricci contraction. In the standard decomposition,
\begin{equation}
\widehat R_{\mathrm{EC}}=C_{\mathrm{EC}}+R^{(2)}+R^{(3)},\qquad
I_{\mathrm{tf}}=I_{\mathrm{W}}+\lVert R^{(2)}\rVert^2+\lVert R^{(3)}\rVert^2.
\tag{TF}\label{eq:TF}
\end{equation}
Here $R^{(2)}$ is pair-antisymmetric and Ricci-tracefree (nine components),
and $R^{(3)}$ is the four-form part (one component). Orthogonality gives the
norm sum. The Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action replaces $I_{\mathrm{W}}$ in (A) by $I_{\mathrm{tf}}$; it assigns the same
quadratic coupling to all three sectors. The irreducible EC-Weyl action sets the latter two
couplings to zero. Neither choice follows from the decomposition alone.

Let $U_{\mathrm{W}}$ and $U_{\mathrm{tf}}$ be the reduced potentials of the irreducible EC-Weyl and Ricci-tracefree EC curvature actions, respectively. On the specified family, (CD) gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lVert R^{(2)}\rVert^2=\lVert R^{(3)}\rVert^2&=\frac{8V^2\eta^2}{3r^2},\\
I_{\mathrm{tf}}-I_{\mathrm{W}}&=\frac{16V^2\eta^2}{3r^2},\\
U_{\mathrm{tf}}-U_{\mathrm{W}}&=-\frac{16}{3}\alpha c_M L V^2\eta^2 r.
\end{aligned}\tag{SHIFT}\label{eq:SHIFT}
\end{equation}
The additional term is independent of shape and of the NY coupling. It
vanishes for $V=0$ or $\eta=0$, and the potentials also agree at $\alpha=0$.
This contribution is determined by the selected curvature sectors and is
independent of the NY coefficient correction discussed in Appendix D.

All three Ricci-tracefree EC curvature potentials follow from (S3),(TN) by replacing $9\eta^2$ by
$\gamma=\eta^2(9-16\kappa^2\alpha V^2)$. The spherical shape difference is
unchanged: at $\alpha\leq0$ the finite round minimum condition becomes
$V\ne0,\gamma<36$, with $r^2=(36-\gamma)/(3V^2)$; the remaining cases follow
from $U_{\mathrm{tf},\mathrm{round}}=c_{S^3}L[V^2r^3+(\gamma-36)r]/(3\kappa^2)$.
At positive coupling the spherical potential is unbounded below over radius and shape.
Nil has the same classification over radius and shape because (SHIFT) has no shape dependence:
at nonpositive coupling $\gamma\geq0$, its zero infimum is unattained;
at positive coupling its inverse-radius term is unbounded below.
For the torus, $\gamma<0$ produces a finite radial minimum at
$r^2=-\gamma/(3V^2)$ for every shape, a degenerate minimum set in radius and shape.
Otherwise the toroidal infimum is zero and unattained unless both torsion
amplitudes vanish, when every radius and shape minimizes. These comparisons
concern a different action; Theorems 1--3 below describe the irreducible EC-Weyl and LC-Weyl actions.

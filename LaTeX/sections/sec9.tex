\section{Variational scope and physical questions}
\label{sec:revised9}

We have held $L,\eta,V$ fixed. If these are varied, complete NY gives additional necessary conditions:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U_M}{\partial V}
=\frac{2c_M L V r^3}{3\kappa^2},
\qquad
\frac{\partial U_M}{\partial\eta}
=\frac{6c_M L\eta r}{\kappa^2},
\qquad
\frac{\partial U_M}{\partial L}=\frac{U_M}{L}.
\tag{VAR}\label{eq:VAR}
\end{equation}
For example, the negative spherical minimum with $V\ne0,\eta^2<4$ has nonzero derivatives with respect to $V$ and $L$. It is not even stationary after those fixed data are released within the reduced family. A positive restricted Hessian is not full EC stability. Conversely, stationarity in all reduced parameters alone would not establish all unreduced field equations.

This distinction locates the shape bound and Hessian as measures of the geometric response within the specified variation space. A full classical solution must satisfy the field equations and constraints, including those for the independent connection.

Dynamical stability requires a treatment of time-dependent perturbations, kinetic terms, and constraints. The Hamiltonian instability considered in Ostrogradsky's theorem\cite{Woodard2015} and boundedness of the present static Euclidean potential are different criteria. Theorem 2 is accordingly a statement about the restricted action at small radius.

Likewise, the self-duality constraint within the spatial two-form subspace in Proposition 1 and the existence of general quantum transition paths are different questions. Analysis of transitions with nonhomogeneous fields and boundary conditions extends beyond the static homogeneous family. Relating comparisons of action values to physical selection also requires common constraints and observables across the sectors being compared.

The derivative identities (VAR) concern the irreducible EC-Weyl and LC-Weyl actions on this family. Equality of restricted potentials does not imply equality of full-field equations or kinetic operators. The Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action is distinct; its extra term (SHIFT) must also be differentiated when varying fixed torsion data.

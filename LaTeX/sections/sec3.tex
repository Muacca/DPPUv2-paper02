\section{Complete NY and curvature two-forms}
\label{sec:revised3}

For (G), the spatial volume form is closed. Direct substitution of (T) gives (Q), using the scalar coefficients defined in (NYD). Simultaneous reversal of the two signed amplitudes leaves their product unchanged.
\begin{equation}
e^a\wedge T_a=\frac{6\eta}{r}e^0\wedge e^1\wedge e^2,
\qquad \mathcal T=\mathcal R=-\frac{4V\eta}{r},\qquad N=0.
\tag{Q}\label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
Thus the complete NY density vanishes on this family and $U$ is independent of $\theta$. This does not assert a vanishing local density for arbitrary position-dependent torsion, actual boundaries, defects, or variable coupling. Using $\mathcal T$ or $\mathcal R$ alone gives distinct actions for component comparisons, rather than the complete NY action.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (curvature forms of the prescribed connection).} The EC connection one-forms for (G),(T) contain only spatial one-forms. Their exterior derivatives and products are spatial two-forms. Hence the circle-form components vanish and $R_{ab}\wedge R_{ab}=0$.

To prove this, the LC and axial contortion one-forms are spatial, while the vector contortion contributes $\omega_{i3}$ proportional to $e^i$. The coefficients are constant in the invariant frame. This is a statement about this connection, not a consequence of a Killing direction alone.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P\,\mathrm{vol}_4&=\sum_{a,b}R_{ab}\wedge R_{ab},
&\mathcal P&=\frac14\epsilon^{cdef}R_{abcd}R_{abef},\\
E_{\mathrm{all}}&=\sum_{a,b,c,d}R_{abcd}^{\,2},
&P_{\mathrm{all}}&=\sum_{a,b,c,d}R_{abcd}(*R)_{abcd}=2\mathcal P,\\
(*R)_{abcd}&=\frac12\epsilon_{cdef}R_{abef}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{P}\label{eq:P}
\end{equation}
Equation (P) uses all-component sums. $E_{\mathrm{all}}$ is a curvature norm, not the Euler density. The form-index Hodge star exchanges spatial and mixed two-forms. Consequently a nonzero curvature in this family is neither self-dual nor anti-self-dual; its two projected norms are equal.

The internal-index dual differs from the form-index dual. The internal contraction $\frac14\epsilon^{abef}R_{abcd}R_{efcd}$ dualizes the first index pair instead of the form indices. At the round point $r=3$, with code inputs $V_\tau=\eta_\tau=1$, it equals $-16/27$, whereas $\mathcal P=0$. Pair-exchange symmetry is not generally available for EC curvature, so (P) uses the form-index dual defined by exterior products. The geometric content of Proposition 1 is that the curvature of the prescribed connection belongs to the spatial two-form subspace.

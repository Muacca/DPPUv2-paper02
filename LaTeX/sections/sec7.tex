\section{Parameter classification and representative slices}
\label{sec:revised7}

\textbf{Theorem 3 (complete spherical classification at fixed torsion parameters).} For $\alpha>0$ all parameter choices are unbounded below. For $\alpha\le0$, (MIN) gives the table. An unattained zero infimum is approached as $r\to0$; the unbounded case with $V=0,\eta^2<4$ follows along the round ray $r\to\infty$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.36\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Fixed data ($\alpha\le0$) & Infimum and attainment \\ \midrule
$V\ne0,\ \eta^2<4$ & Unique negative global minimum (MIN) \\
$V\ne0,\ \eta^2\ge4$ & Zero infimum, unattained \\
$V=0,\ \eta^2<4$ & Unbounded below \\
$V=0,\ \eta^2=4$ & Zero minimum on the entire round ray \\
$V=0,\ \eta^2>4$ & Zero infimum, unattained \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
At $V=0,\eta^2=4$, the potential is identically zero on the round ray; there is no isolated radial minimum. The sign of $\alpha$ alone therefore determines neither existence of a minimum nor boundedness for all parameters on its nonpositive side.

The competition between the cubic term controlled by $V$ and the linear term controlled by $\eta$ determines the radial extrema. When $V$ vanishes the cubic term is absent, changing the $\eta^2<4$ case from a finite-radius minimum to a large-radius runaway. At $\eta^2=4$ the linear term vanishes: nonzero $V$ gives monotonic increase, while $V=0$ leaves the entire round ray as the minimum set.

The next table fixes $V_\tau=4$, $L=\kappa=1$ and compares amplitudes inside, on, and outside the threshold. The round values are common to all nonpositive $\alpha$, and reversing the sign of $\eta$ leaves the potential unchanged.

The $\eta_\tau=0$ slice realizes the finite-radius minimum in (MIN); $\eta_\tau=\pm2$ lies on the threshold, and $\eta_\tau=-3$ outside it. Appendix D displays these curves together with their relation to the preceding version's reference expression.

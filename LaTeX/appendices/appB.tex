\section{Reproduction and classification outputs}
\label{sec:revised12}

The public entry point is \texttt{script/README.md}. The render command of \texttt{reproduce\_figures.py} draws Figures 1--4 from the supplied data; recompute uses the public engine to write six figure/table CSV files, parameters, and images to a new directory. Only the Appendix D comparison curve uses the preceding NY expression with the current spherical volume. The three geometry runners and two symbolic supplements reproduce the potentials, LC-Weyl expression, and EC curvature block structure. The conventions guide specifies indices, signs, global geometry and shape; the implementation guide explains symbolic computation and APIs, and the classification guide defines scan/viewer outputs. The public library supports these current reproduction paths; the scientific arguments are given in the paper and appendices.

The scan and viewer share a classifier. For complete NY, it separately reports the Sections 7--8 classification on all $r,x>0$ and the radial classification on all $r>0$ at fixed shape. Torsion amplitudes, $L$ and $\kappa$ are fixed; the CLI/viewer use $L=\kappa=1$. Finite minima, unattained infima, degenerate minimum sets, and unbounded potentials are distinguished. A round spherical slice can have a minimum at positive $\alpha$ while the domain with both radius and shape varying is unbounded. A Nil minimum at fixed shape is not a minimum over radius and shape. Actions using $\mathcal T$ or $\mathcal R$ alone are outside this classification, but their potential curves remain available for comparison.

For fixed shape, (S3),(TN) give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U(r)&=p(Ar^3+Br+D/r),\quad p=\frac{c_ML}{3\kappa^2}>0,\quad A=V^2,\\
(B,D)_{S^3}&=(9\eta^2-12f(x),-1024\kappa^2\alpha x^2(1-x^3)^2),\\
(B,D)_{T^3}&=(9\eta^2,0),\\
(B,D)_{\mathrm{Nil}^3}&=(9\eta^2+3x^4/4,-4\kappa^2\alpha x^8),\\
U'(r)=0&\iff 3Ay^2+By-D=0,\quad y=r^2>0.
\end{aligned}\tag{RAD}
\end{equation}
The second derivative at positive roots and the limits at zero and infinity determine the infimum and attainment. If $D<0$, the small-radius potential is unbounded below even when a local radial minimum exists. If $A=B=D=0$, all radii are degenerate minima. Plot windows do not discard roots; numerical grids only visualize classifications.

Inputs are treated as their represented floating-point values, without rounding to two decimal places. The boundaries $V=0$, $|\eta|=2$ and $\alpha=0$ are not identified with neighbouring values. Fixed-shape coefficients and roots use cancellation-resistant expressions and high-precision arithmetic, with unresolved boundaries and unrepresentable numeric values explicitly flagged. The viewer displays current engine curves alongside both domain classifications, infima and radii. These describe restricted potentials, not full-field vacua or Lorentzian ghosts. The classification guide in the attached script directory specifies the CSV fields and their classification domains.

The public selectors are \texttt{EC\_IRR} (default) for the irreducible EC-Weyl action, \texttt{LC} for the LC-Weyl action, and \texttt{EC\_TRACEFREE} for the Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action. The six CSVs, parameters and four figures retain their LC provenance; recomputation explicitly selects LC. By (ID) they also apply to the irreducible EC-Weyl action on this family. The Ricci-tracefree EC curvature action replaces $9\eta^2$ by $\gamma$ in (RAD), with the classification in Section~\ref{sec:ricci-tracefree}. At its toroidal minimum every shape minimizes: the CSV field \texttt{global\_r} records the common radius and \texttt{global\_x} is empty. Threshold signs use exact rational arithmetic on the supplied binary floats before numerical evaluation.

The torsion selectors allow the axial component alone (\texttt{AX}, $V=0$), the vector-trace component alone (\texttt{VT}, $\eta=0$), or both components (\texttt{MX}). Zero amplitudes are allowed in all modes. Independently, \texttt{FULL} uses $\mathcal T-\mathcal R$ from (NYD), while \texttt{TT} and \texttt{REE} use $+\mathcal T$ and $+\mathcal R$, respectively, multiplied by the real coupling $\theta$. The plus sign denotes the component convention, not a restriction on $\theta$. In particular, the standalone \texttt{REE} choice does not include the minus sign in the complete NY form.
